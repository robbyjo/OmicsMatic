Writing a coherent human omics manuscript

Turn linked scientific evidence into a coherent, reviewable manuscript.

Template: 2026-11-14.6. The executable source of the complete prompt is manuscript-writing-v1.txt. StudyManuscriptPolicy loads that resource for drafting and editorial review and records its version and SHA-256. This guide explains the rationale, the paper survey, and how to inspect the implemented behavior. The prompt is a writing specification; statistical definitions and frozen study evidence remain authoritative.

What the supplied Splicing-BMI manuscript showed

The reviewed PDF was the October 7, 2026, 12-page revision supplied by the user. This review concerns that document's writing and presentation, not whether its historical analysis is the current scientific result.

  • The Introduction moves immediately from transcript regulation to EPB41, PCBP2, Runx1 and pancreatic cells. The reader has not yet been told the broader adiposity problem, what human transcriptomic studies have established, or which unresolved question requires an isoform-level measurement.
  • The next paragraphs organize themselves around individual gene papers. They meet paragraph-role labels but do not form a persuasive argument from background to question to design. A collection of relevant references is not a rationale.
  • Generic contrasts and qualifications recur in almost every section. Some limitations are essential, especially model comparability, heterogeneity and analysis-population uncertainty. Their repetition as broad disclaimers obscures the specific implications that the reader actually needs.
  • Editorial reasons, source-status statements, parameter-review headings and software audit commentary appear among scientific paragraphs. Printing collapsed HTML details exposes additional labels. Those are useful review records but interrupt an article.
  • Table 1 exists on page 5. It describes phenotype-file populations and is explicitly provisional. It does not establish membership in each association analysis. A study-population table must identify its population, not merely occupy a numbered table slot. The software also had a separate gap: the study-wide AI draft did not directly receive local participant-summary outputs.

Improved prose cannot repair historical sample membership, model specifications, or statistical results. Those need their own verified inputs. The attached PDF was not rewritten or treated as the current study contract.

Structural survey of twelve human transcriptomics papers

This is a purposive editorial comparison, not a systematic review or a scientific quality ranking. It covers phenotype-association studies, blood transcriptomics, multi-tissue resources and regulatory-genetics studies from 2010 through 2025. Introductions/backgrounds, section order, and population reporting were inspected in full-text public articles; Discussion framing was inspected where relevant. Ten articles were retrieved through Europe PMC XML. The FHS RNA-source study and GTEx 2020 article were inspected through PMC web text; GTEx 2017 was inspected through both XML and web text. Retrieval was on November 14, 2026. Inaccessible candidate papers were not counted. No private manuscript text was sent to a model or external literature service.

The notes below are original observations about organization, not statements that the papers support the new study's specific findings. Their scientific claims, historical prevalence estimates, novelty claims and causal terminology should not be copied into another manuscript.

Its main Table 1 summarizes associations, so our population-Table-1 requirement is a deliberate product choice, not a claimed universal journal rule.

The recurring editorial lesson is problem -> relevant knowledge -> consequential gap -> measurement/design rationale -> objective. The precise order of the two middle functions varies naturally. This is an inference from the comparison, not a formally measured readability result. None of the examples justifies copying a rigid paragraph count, a claim of novelty, or stronger causal language.

Detailed template and composition process

Use the complete runtime prompt for drafting. It covers preparation, title/abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results/Table 1, Discussion, caveat placement, style, and evidence/output rules. The following describes how to apply it in a study.

1. Prepare an argument, not a list of sections

Before drafting, fill these questions from reviewed evidence:

  1. What biological or clinical problem makes the question worth asking?
  2. What do the closest human studies already tell us?
  3. What exact uncertainty remains, and why does it matter?
  4. What biological process are we trying to understand, what do we actually measure, and why is that measure a suitable proxy for this question?
  5. Why can this tissue, population and design address that uncertainty?
  6. What are the declared primary objectives and secondary/exploratory questions?
  7. What could resolving the question change in scientific understanding or enable in subsequent research, and why does that make the study worth doing?
  8. What answer do the actual results support, with what material limitations?

Keep this outline in the working/review records. Test the logical connections before writing polished sentences. If the only available literature consists of hit-gene papers, request broader background and directly comparable human studies. Do not assemble a background from whatever citations happen to be available. Preserve the scientist's stated motivation. If it is missing from the reviewed context, request it explicitly; neither a phenotype label nor a list of analysis goals establishes why the study was conceived. This writing change does not add unreviewed private project prose to external model packets.

Persuasive motivation and potential impact are required. The reader should understand why the question needs answering, why this measurement/design is useful for answering it, and what a credible answer could teach us or make possible. Evidence and a chain of reasoning earn the importance; stronger adjectives do not. Potential impact is a prospective scientific contribution, not a result preview or an unsupported clinical promise. Mechanical paragraph-role checks cannot certify any of these qualities.

2. Introduction: default five-paragraph plan

Paragraph 1: biological problem. Establish the phenotype and the specific biological question. Explain why understanding it matters. Avoid a stock opening followed by a sudden jump into a gene. Current prevalence figures require current sources and are optional; they do not substitute for a scientific rationale.

Paragraph 2: knowledge and molecular resolution. Explain what relevant human studies have found. Introduce gene abundance, transcript usage or the appropriate measurement in accessible terms. Explain why this measurement could reveal information not represented by the earlier measurements.

Paragraph 3: question-to-measurement bridge. Connect the biological question to the process of interest and measured quantity or proxy. Explain why this choice is informative and how its interpretability helps address the question. Compare a relevant alternative fairly and only on supported dimensions. Tissue, sampling and replication strengthen this argument; they must not become a substitute for the scientific motivation.

Paragraph 4: consequential gap and potential impact. Use the closest studies to explain what remains unknown about the same question. State what resolving it could change or enable: distinguish explanations, link an association to a specific molecular feature, or guide a discriminating experiment. The anticipated impact must follow from the measurement and gap. A free-standing paragraph about possible applications is not that bridge. Include contrary evidence where it changes the rationale; assay or sample-size differences alone do not establish novelty.

Paragraph 5: close the motivating argument. State the declared biological objective, the chosen measurement, and the broad design used to address the gap. The reader should see how the anticipated contribution follows from this design. Keep hypothesis history accurate. Avoid detailed analysis settings and a preview of selected genes. A discovery or resource paper can have objectives rather than a fabricated prespecified directional hypothesis.

Four paragraphs are often enough by combining related functions. Longer sections are permitted when justified. The four existing role names remain supported; the first role can contain problem and knowledge paragraphs. The role order is still checked, but paragraph counts are planning targets. Topic sentences read together must form an argument. Repeated transition words cannot repair unrelated content.

Splicing-BMI: the clarified scientific motivation

The scientist clarified that the motivating question is whether splicing plays a role in BMI/obesity biology. Isoform ratios were chosen as an interpretable proxy because an association can be related directly to current annotated transcripts. The previous example overemphasized blood sampling and reproducibility, without making those choices serve this biological question. The revised argument is:

  1. Splicing could be relevant to adiposity, so understanding its phenotype relationship is a worthwhile biological question.
  2. Studying that question requires a measurable representation of transcript processing beyond total gene abundance.
  3. Relative usage of annotated transcripts provides a proxy with transcript-level interpretability, supporting hypotheses about specific RNA products and their follow-up. Junction/intron-event methods offer a complementary representation.
  4. It remains to be established which of these transcript-interpretable changes relate to BMI/obesity in the study populations. Resolving that question could connect broad phenotype associations to specific RNA-processing hypotheses and make subsequent functional experiments more focused.
  5. The objectives and cohort design follow from that question and expected contribution; replication and genetic follow-up support its investigation.

This is a scientist-supplied motivation, not a claim that the study was prospectively registered with a directional hypothesis or that its results prove causal involvement. The transcript-level interpretation rationale is consistent with the isoform-ratio study. The LeafCutter paper explains the complementary value of annotation-free intron-excision quantification. These sources support a question-specific measurement choice, not universal superiority, inability to interpret junctions, or an unperformed head-to-head comparison. Ratios also reflect annotation/quantification choices and other RNA processing mechanisms; investigating a role for splicing is distinct from having established a causal mechanism. “Instrumental” in the motivating question should not be mistaken for the instrumental-variable assumptions of MR.

Keep the scientific question constant through the last three paragraphs. Test each transition explicitly: why does this question require this measurement; why does that measurement make the remaining gap consequential; and how does the final objective address it? Read those paragraphs without the Results. If they could be reordered freely, or explain only what was done rather than why it was worth doing, revise them before accepting the Introduction.

3. Methods and participant characteristics

Describe cohorts, actual visits, selection and tissue first, followed by molecular measurement, QC and performed analyses. Give the exact estimand, scale, model, covariates, relatedness adjustment, multiple-testing and replication definitions. State software versions, resource versions and effective parameters. Include recorded reasons for unusual choices. Avoid turning standard choices into an extended defense or mixing execution history into scientific prose.

Table 1 should identify its population and include cohort/design-stage columns, analysis N and unit, age, the recorded sex/gender variable, main phenotype, relevant covariates, units, denominators and missingness. Native summaries currently support means/sample SDs and binary proportions; the prompt must not invent medians/IQRs or categories unavailable from the supplied artifacts. Those would require a separate supported summary calculation. Population-specific or feature-specific sample sizes must be distinguished from a shared overall N.

Missing summaries produce a visible Table 1 placeholder and needs_input review. Unverified phenotype-file populations stay labeled provisional. Confirmed sample membership and completeness are separate questions. The table is inserted locally from retained summary artifacts. Participant rows and demographic values are not automatically added to a model packet by this change.

4. Results: people and question before genes

Open with the population, analytical scope and Table 1. Present primary evidence and replication before downstream interpretation, following Methods order. Within each result, orient the reader to the comparison, give the estimate and uncertainty, and report the robustness result that matters. Use explicit denominators and distinguish transcripts, genes, loci and participants. Keep important null or discordant results visible. Do not turn Results into a ranking of P-values, a sequence of operation status messages, or a literature review.

5. Discussion: a persuasive synthesis and a justified next step

Begin by answering the study question. Develop the selected principal findings in a biologically meaningful order. Each may span several connected paragraphs. The observation, literature, mechanism, counterevidence, alternatives, relevance, next experiments and limitations are reasoning functions, not mandatory repeated subheadings. Preserve the full discussion in supplements when necessary while keeping the main argument understandable on its own.

Persuasiveness must come from the reasoning and evidence. Return to the Introduction's motivation and explain the specific knowledge added relative to the closest primary studies: what can now be understood, distinguished or tested? Replication, refinement and informative null results can all be worthwhile contributions. Novelty adjectives, lists of significant results and unsupported clinical promises do not establish scientific value.

Tie the findings together rather than summarize each analysis in isolation. Explain agreement, complementary information and unresolved tension, and how the combination changes the answer to the study question. Account for shared samples and dependent analyses; do not count them as independent confirmation or force discordant findings into a single mechanism. The conclusion should follow from this synthesis and answer the question that motivated the Introduction.

Highlight something unexpected only if an observed result departs from a documented hypothesis or a source-supported prior expectation. Explain the expectation, the departure, its scientific implication, alternative biological or methodological explanations, and the test that would distinguish them. Label post hoc explanations. If the evidence does not support a surprise, omit that claim; the study need not be surprising to be worthwhile.

The default closing sequence is implications → limitations → prioritized next steps → conclusion. Anchor potential impact in an actual finding and explain what it could enable under a meaningful condition. Discuss grounded weaknesses constructively: for each material gap, identify the affected inference and what remains supported, then link feasible follow-up to that uncertainty. Explain how different outcomes would change the interpretation and which constraints would remain. Some weaknesses can only be reduced or require another design. Do not promise to overcome all limitations or replace them with generic pessimism. Prioritize follow-up by scientific value and the most consequential uncertainty; proposed work must remain clearly prospective.

For Splicing-BMI, the Discussion should return to the motivating question about splicing in BMI/obesity biology and the interpretability of isoform ratios against annotated transcripts. Explain how the actual supplied findings advance that question together, what the measurement resolves and what it leaves uncertain. Do not convert associations into proof that splicing causes obesity, or transcript interpretability into demonstrated superiority over junction methods. Any proposed functional, longitudinal or cross-assay follow-up should address a specific unresolved link in the observed evidence, rather than appear as a generic wish list. This example defines the argument; it supplies no new study result.

Give a specific limitation at the point where it changes interpretation, and consolidate shared limitations. For example:

Habit to avoid Better editorial action
Repeating that associations do not establish causation after every finding Use association-appropriate verbs throughout and explain the actual design limitation and its consequence in one developed passage.
Announcing that the second finding matters because it occupies an impact slot State the biological interpretation and the evidence that makes it consequential. Keep impact ranking in review metadata.
Repeating that a protein paper is not validation of an isoform ratio Explain once which molecular level each study measured, what connection is supported, and which specific experiment would test the remaining link.
Adding a drug-target disclaimer without any therapeutic claim Remove the gratuitous therapeutic framing through an attributed edit. Retain genuine constraints when therapeutic implications are discussed.
Replacing a source-specific limitation with vague hedging State the concrete problem, such as incomplete regional coverage, and its effect on the inference. Preserve any mandatory caveat.

These examples are editing instructions, not factual claims about the supplied study. The renderer never deletes cautionary sentences from authored text. It moves automatically generated editorial labels into a review copy. Rewriting an existing manuscript still requires an attributed revision request.

6. Mathematical notation

Use concise numerical display precision in all manuscript sections, including literal prose and equations. Structured tables and evidence bindings already use StudyReportNumbers: normally two decimal places, with three significant figures in scientific notation for small or large values. Apply the same writing rule to unbound prose; keep justified precision where needed to interpret small effects or correlations, and never turn a nonzero P value into zero.

For example, describe a scaling constant as approximately 1.48, rather than printing 1.482602218505602; its full precision remains in the implementation and provenance. Exact decision cutoffs, counts, coordinates, identifiers and source exports remain unchanged. The typography review flags decimal literals with at least four fractional digits and more than four significant digits in prose and equations, excluding code, URLs and embedded identifiers. This is a review cue, not a blind rounding operation: revise an estimate or approximate constant in context, and preserve a deliberately exact threshold. The cue does not certify all short values or text embedded in figure images; inspect the PDF as well.

Use ≤ and ≥ in scientific prose. The renderer also converts their ASCII spellings in prose without changing the authored revision, evidence or numeric values. Strict inequalities remain strict; code and URLs are preserved.

Author inline equations with \(...\) and display equations with \[...\] (or $$...$$). Single-dollar delimiters are not supported, to avoid confusing currency with math. Use TeX fractions, summations, roots, Greek letters, subscripts and superscripts. For example, only when this is the actual pooling method:

\[\hat{\beta}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{K}w_i\hat{\beta}_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{K}w_i},\qquad
  w_i=\frac{1}{\mathrm{SE}(\hat{\beta}_i)^2},\qquad
  \mathrm{SE}(\hat{\beta})=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{K}w_i}}.\]

Define the index, number of cohorts, estimates and standard errors. This replaces sum(w beta)/sum(w) with an actual summation and fraction; it does not authorize changing the estimator or inventing missing definitions. Use notation such as \(P \le 5\times10^{-8}\), \(r^2\) and \(30\,\mathrm{kg}/\mathrm{m}^{2}\) for thresholds, powers and units. In JSON, escape each backslash; YAML single-quoted or block strings preserve TeX backslashes. Keep citations outside math delimiters.

Native revisions, their supplements and AI drafts embed KaTeX 0.19.0 and render native MathML locally, using the browser's math font. No remote scripts, fonts or model calls are required. The pinned library, MIT license and verified distribution hashes are under src/main/resources/org/jlinalg/study/math/katex/. Its rendering options support MathML output and bounded, untrusted input; links, images and HTML commands are rejected here.

The mathematics_review records equations and flags raw formula pseudocode or unmatched delimiters. Ambiguous inherited formulas require an attributed edit after checking the source method. Unsupported TeX remains visible with an error instead of being silently removed. Inspect the rendered HTML in a current browser with MathML support, then print/export to PDF after every equation has data-math-status="complete" and the document has data-manuscript-math="complete". Check the PDF visually for glyphs and clipping. A text-only HTML-to-PDF converter that does not run scripts and render MathML cannot export these equations correctly. Typesetting does not certify mathematics.

Implementation and version behavior

The main manuscript presents the science. Internal version histories, template versions, hashes, audit trails and execution receipts stay in separate review records. The reading copy has no audit navigation or template-version banner. The scientific supplement contains authored supplementary notes and participant details, plus one short link to supporting records. Full operation manifests and audit listings remain in evidence-review.html (or publication-evidence-review.html for native scaffolds). Use one concise Data and code availability statement with verified locations and actual access conditions near the end. Software/resource versions and technical reproducibility details belong in Supplementary Methods or linked documentation; an optional provenance paragraph in the scientific supplement should be brief. Existing authored prose is preserved until an attributed edit relocates it; the renderer does not silently strip sentences.

The Discussion should be constructively optimistic: connect a finding to what it could enable, why that prospect matters and what specific validation would advance it. Prefer a meaningful condition such as “If confirmed in an independent sample, this pattern could help distinguish…” to stacked hedges and repeated disclaimers. The implication must have an evidence anchor. Keep consequential limitations and end with the supported contribution and a promising next question.

Write the structured abstract after the body is finalized, then insert it once after the title, before Introduction. Use Background, Methods, Results, Conclusion. It must reconcile the objective, design, population, principal quantitative findings and implication with the finished article. The software checks structure and flags an unchanged abstract when a native revision changes the rendered body; it cannot prove authorship chronology or scientific accuracy. Native requests use four section: abstract blocks with abstract_role values background, methods, results, conclusion. A legacy unstructured abstract is preserved and flagged. Missing abstracts render as four labeled input placeholders, with abstract-review.yaml reporting needs_input.

For example, append the four abstract blocks to a revision request after writing the body (the renderer places them first):

- id: abstract-background
  section: abstract
  abstract_role: background
  text: "[SCIENTIST INPUT REQUIRED: biological problem, gap and objective.]"
  sources: []
  rationale: Summarize the finalized Introduction.

Repeat for methods, results and conclusion using the matching final content. Use section: availability for the short availability statement. Abstract and availability text are excluded from the main-text word budget. Absent availability information remains an input placeholder; no repository or public deposit is invented.

Review before using a generated article

Read the Introduction without the Results at hand. It should make sense to a colleague unfamiliar with the selected genes. Then read only the topic sentences, followed by the first Results paragraph and Table 1. Ask whether the motivation, question, participants, measurement and contribution are clear. Check that every important limitation remains, while generic disclaimers and audit commentary no longer dominate. Read the Discussion's opening and conclusion together: do they answer the motivating question and make the contribution clear? Then check how the findings support that answer, whether any claimed surprise has a documented expectation, and why the proposed next step follows from the evidence and its limits. Finally verify claims, values and citations against their sources.

The automated tests exercise prompt routing/versioning, paragraph flexibility, table insertion and escaping, provisional populations, and preservation of prose and audit artifacts. They do not establish that a live model will write a good manuscript. That requires a generated draft and substantive human review.
